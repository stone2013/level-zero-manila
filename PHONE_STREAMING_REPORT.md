## Phone

- One initial phone, ID `phone-1`, starts with 100% charge and occupies 1×1 inventory cell at `(0, 1)`. It is appended after the four foods and two original water bottles so their identities remain stable. Food remains 1×1; water remains 1×2.
- The phone is a real inventory/world object. Selecting it offers “查看资料,” never consumption. The existing drag, arrange, drop-path, occupancy and pickup-capacity checks apply. Drop/pickup preserves the same ID and exact battery value; full backpacks cannot remove a world phone.
- A small local SVG represents the phone in the backpack, and a shallow dark phone mesh represents it on the ground. No imported copyrighted wiki illustrations or text were used.
- The offline reader contains three brief, original in-game notes: Level 0, Manila, and an incomplete unknown-exit record. The UI identifies these as incomplete local notes about this game's adaptation. They do not offer GPS, a map, live connectivity, a flashlight, a camera, or playable new levels.
- Opening the reader pauses exploration just like the backpack. Only the device clock continues while the game is active in the foreground. Device time is advanced exactly once by the app frame, independently of world simulation; survival, movement, door animation and approach timers remain frozen inside the backpack/reader.
- Unplugged reading drains `100 / 480` percentage points per active second: approximately eight minutes from 100% to zero. Closing the reader stops drain. At zero, chapter contents are hidden, navigation is disabled and a charging hint appears; this does not end the run.
- Manual pause, settings, note, menu, end states, background, page hide, window blur, the mobile portrait gate and world-loading waits freeze device time. Background and blur use the existing manual-resume behavior; no missed time is caught up. Frame delta is capped to 0.05 seconds.
- The Manila bench has a fixed cable from a wall socket. Its compact render geometry adds no obstacle or moving-cable simulation. Interacting nearby connects the held phone; the reader has an explicit connect/disconnect button when within 1.3 metres of the cable endpoint with clear line of sight.
- A cable can bind only one concrete phone ID. Repeated connect is idempotent. Charging adds `100 / 40` percentage points per second, capped at 100%; a powered reader uses the socket instead of draining the battery, so empty-to-full remains approximately 40 active seconds. Charging continues in the backpack/reader by design, with this behavior explained in settings and the reader.
- Leaving the cable's local range/room or dropping the connected phone disconnects immediately. No remote charging and no copied phones. Menu/background pause keeps a valid physical connection but contributes zero charge until explicit resumption.
- Esc/I first closes the phone to the backpack; the next closes the backpack. Tab stays within the visible reader's controls. No world key actions pass through the reader. Short landscape layouts scroll the notes while retaining 44px close and charging buttons and safe-area padding.

## Streaming integration

Integrated `dist/world.js` extends ordinary maze generation to all four directions, with reciprocal portals across positive and negative chunk coordinates. The original central maze, spatial folds and guaranteed ordinary Manila route remain. Room/item classification checks the actual room rectangle, so arbitrary eastern chunks do not become Manila. One finite seven-item ledger survives layout and rendering eviction.

The initial draft's 29 m fog was rejected before delivery because it reduced the user's approved clear visibility. The final candidate keeps fog at 16/80 m, vertical FOV at 76° and far plane at 65 m. It requests the actual Three.js view frustum instead of treating the far plane as a circular radius. Initial views load asynchronously; incomplete later views retain the last complete presentation and a non-focus-stealing loading status. Exploration and device clocks freeze during this wait. The fold destination is requested even when behind the camera; an unready landing rolls the crossing back instead of silently losing a transfer.

Runtime limits: 16 procedural layout chunks; 35 m render regions of 7×7 cells; at most 49 ready/pending regions (absolute maximum 2,401 cell floors), with required views prioritized and a bounded LRU for previous views. Loading uses an 8 ms / 48-step soft per-frame budget; normal exploration uses 3 ms / 24 steps. These are scheduling targets, not preemptive guarantees. Aspect ratios above 4:1 or an over-cap view get an explicit resize hint; visible regions are never silently omitted. Local geometry, UVs and camera rebasing are checked at ±10 km. The user allowed revisited areas to differ; this version uses coordinate-seeded ordinary layouts while retaining the original spatial connection changes.

This preserves the existing lighting, wallpaper and camera settings, but increases the bounded working set. Initial loading and fast turning may wait longer. No real-phone performance claim is made. `CLEAR_VIEW_REPORT.md` supersedes the earlier standalone streaming report's region size and fog discussion.
